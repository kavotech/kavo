Best CMS for enterprise marketing teams: Why Craft CMS stands out in 2026

9th September 2026

Roughly a 11 minute read by

Navneet

rich-text

Choosing the best CMS for enterprise teams is rarely just a CMS decision.

At enterprise scale, a CMS sits right at the heart of your marketing ecosystem. It's the connective tissue between teams, channels, data and performance. Pick the wrong one and you feel it everywhere – sluggish content workflows, brand execution that drifts out of line, technical ceilings that hold campaigns back, and SEO performance that never quite gets where it should.

Here's the thing, though: most enterprise teams aren't struggling because they chose a bad platform. They're struggling because the platform doesn't match how the business actually runs. It’s less ‘we bought a lemon’ and more ‘we bought a lovely three-piece suite for a house with no room to put it in’. Which reframes the whole question. "What's the best CMS for enterprise?" isn't really about which platform is most popular, but about which one fits your complexity, your structure, and your ambitions.

What defines the best CMS for enterprise?

There's no single answer, but there is a consistent set of requirements.

At this level, a CMS has to do considerably more than publish content. It has to support scale without piling on friction, which means:

  • Flexible content modelling rather than rigid, locked-down templates
  • Strong governance and granular user permissions
  • Clean integration with the wider stack: CRM, analytics, ecommerce
  • Room to evolve without forcing a full rebuild every few years

Performance across multiple sites and regions doesn't make that list as a bonus feature. At enterprise scale, it's simply the baseline. Fall short on any of these, and the cracks start showing soon enough.

Why many enterprise CMS platforms fall short

On paper, most enterprise platforms pledge the same four things: flexibility, scalability, personalisation, performance. In the real world, they tend to stumble into familiar traps.

Some lean so hard into structure that marketing teams can't move. Every change turns into a development ticket. Every campaign needs a workaround. There's plenty of control, just no flexibility to go with it.

Others swing the other way. Endlessly configurable, hugely powerful and a genuine grind to manage from one day to the next. Teams end up spending more time wrestling the CMS than extracting any value from it. All the gears in the world, and nobody quite able to drive.

A lot of enterprise CMS decisions get made on technical requirements alone, which is fine right up until marketing teams start working with the thing. Then the friction surfaces: slow publishing, limited editorial control, a reliance on developers for changes that ought to take a couple of minutes.

And then there's the money. Licensing is one line on the invoice. Long-term cost of ownership is the one that bites. Platforms leaning on constant developer input, elaborate integrations or a growing pile of workarounds get expensive over time. Not because they're bad platforms, but because they were never the right fit to begin with.

What enterprise marketing teams actually need

Boil it right down and most enterprise teams are juggling three things at once:

  • Control – brand consistency, governance, permissions
  • Flexibility – campaigns, content, speed to market
  • Scalability – multiple regions, teams and channels

The best CMS for enterprise is the one that holds all three in balance. Not flawlessly. Nothing manages that. But practically, in a way that holds up under real pressure.

Where Craft CMS fits for enterprise teams

This is the point where Craft CMS tends to pull ahead. Not because it's the biggest name on the shortlist, or the most familiar, but because of how it's put together.

Its content modelling is flexible in the truest sense. No predefined templates, no forced page types. Just real content structures shaped around how your business works. Fields, components, relationships, all defined by you, which is precisely what lets enterprise teams scale without duplicating effort or scattering inconsistent experiences across sites and regions.

For organisations running multiple brands, domains or markets, Craft's multisite capability is built for exactly that brand of complexity. A structured multisite setup gives you central control where you want it and local flexibility where you need it, without the sprawl that creeps in on platforms where multisite was clearly an afterthought. 

The editorial experience deserves a mention too, because a CMS can be technically immaculate and still fall flat if the teams using it can't get on with it. A Ferrari is no use if it takes three developers and a fortnight to change gear. Craft is built with content teams front of mind: a clean interface, clear workflows, controlled permissions that cut the reliance on developers for routine updates without loosening governance. In our experience, that balance is one of the biggest factors in whether a CMS delivers on its promise over the long haul.

On the technical side, Craft hands development teams full control over templates, front-end performance and infrastructure. Performance is engineered rather than inherited. And at enterprise scale, where the gap between fast and slow is measurable in revenue, that distinction earns its keep.

And since no enterprise CMS lives on an island, Craft is built to integrate cleanly with CRMs, analytics platforms, ecommerce systems and marketing tools. Its API-first approach means it slots into a complex digital ecosystem rather than demanding everything bend around it. You can read more about our Craft CMS services here.

Craft vs Sitecore vs AEM vs WordPress VIP

When enterprise teams sit down to compare options, these four come up the most. Here's how they stack up in practice.
 

rich-text

Craft CMS vs Sitecore

Sitecore tends to be pitched as a fully integrated digital experience platform, and it's genuinely powerful, especially where personalisation and enterprise-grade marketing automation top the priority list. That power has a price, though: hefty implementation and licensing overhead, a heavy reliance on development teams, and slower lead times on campaigns.

Craft comes at it differently. It doesn't try to be everything to everyone. It concentrates on doing the CMS layer exceptionally well, with flexibility, performance and usability at the core. If you're already deep in the Microsoft ecosystem, or advanced personalisation at scale is a real priority, Sitecore stacks up.

For most others, Craft is quicker to implement, friendlier for content teams and noticeably lighter on long-term operational cost.

Craft CMS vs Adobe Experience Manager (AEM)

AEM is a true heavyweight. The platform of choice for global enterprises wrestling complex content, asset management and governance demands. It's excellent at digital asset management at scale, deep enterprise integration and large-scale content governance.

The trade-off is significant: a high cost of ownership, a steep learning curve and slower execution on marketing workflows. Craft offers a leaner alternative: simpler content modelling, faster publishing, more agility for the marketing team. AEM earns its place in organisations that genuinely need tightly integrated DAM and CMS capability at global scale, backed by sizeable internal technical teams.

For everyone else, it can be a bit like taking a lorry to do the weekly shop - more platform than the job calls for.

Craft CMS vs WordPress VIP

WordPress VIP is often the default enterprise pick. Familiar, well supported by a strong ecosystem, with managed hosting that lifts some of the infrastructure burden. It’s the comfortable, sensible choice – the digital equivalent of slippers on, a brew and a biscuit. It does well with content-heavy sites, teams already at home in WordPress, and projects where speed to launch is the priority. Push it to enterprise scale, though, and the limitations start to show: heavy plugin reliance, content structure that drifts across large sites, and technical debt quietly stacking up.

Craft brings cleaner architecture, structured content modelling that leans on plugins far less, and stronger long-term scalability. Which way you lean really depends on how embedded the WordPress ecosystem already is in how your teams work, and how much that weighs against build quality over the long term.

So which enterprise CMS is right for you?

There's no universal winner here, only the right fit for your organisation. If you want an all-in-one marketing suite and have the budget and technical resource to feed it, Sitecore or AEM can make real sense. If familiarity and fast deployment matter most, WordPress VIP is the usual default. And if you're after flexibility, performance and control without the complexity tax, Craft is well worth a serious look.

How to choose the right CMS for your organisation

This is where most decisions come unstuck, and it's rarely the platform's fault. It's the process used to pick it.

Start with structure, not features. Before you compare a single platform, get clear on how your content is structured, how your teams actually work, what needs to scale and where the pain points already are. Without that clarity, every platform looks viable and the decision defaults to whatever's familiar or carries the biggest name.

Get marketing and technical teams in the room together from the start. Enterprise CMS decisions that tilt too far one way produce platforms that look great on paper and create friction in practice. The best outcomes come from genuine alignment – usability and workflow needs on one side, architecture and performance requirements on the other.

Plan for growth, not just launch. A CMS ought to absorb new markets, new brands and new content requirements without triggering yet another rebuild. The platforms that age gracefully are the ones chosen with that future in mind.

And weigh total cost of ownership, not just the licence fee. The real figure folds in development time, ongoing maintenance, workflow efficiency and the opportunity cost of slow execution. Craft's lower licensing overhead means more of the budget can go into custom functionality that genuinely sets the brand apart, rather than disappearing into platform fees.

Common mistakes when choosing an enterprise CMS

A handful of patterns crop up again and again:

  • Choosing on brand rather than fit. A big name guarantees nothing about the outcome.
  • Over-prioritising features. More capability counts for little if the team can't actually use it.
  • Underestimating governance. Without clear ownership and defined workflows, even the best platform drifts into inconsistency over time.
  • Leaving SEO and performance until after the build. Both should shape the decision from the outset, not get retrofitted once it's too late to matter.

So, what is the best CMS for enterprise?

The honest answer is that it depends on how your organisation operates. But the platforms that consistently exceed expectations share the same DNA: flexible enough to adapt, structured enough to scale, fast enough to compete, and usable enough for the people working in them every single day.

That's where Craft keeps standing out. Not by promising everything, but by letting you build exactly what you need, without the overhead that comes with platforms designed for organisations far larger or more complex than yours.

Frequently asked questions

accordion

What is the best CMS for enterprise organisations?

It depends on your structure, teams and requirements. Craft CMS, Adobe Experience Manager and Sitecore are all widely used at enterprise level, but the right call comes down to how well the platform supports your workflows, your content model and your growth plans – not which name carries the most weight.

What features should an enterprise CMS have?

Flexible content modelling, strong governance controls, clean integration with the wider stack, performance optimisation and multi-site support are the consistent ones. Usability for non-technical teams matters every bit as much as technical capability – a platform content teams struggle with will underperform whatever its feature list says.

Is Craft CMS suitable for enterprise businesses?

Yes. Craft suits enterprise organisations well thanks to its flexibility, scalability and API-first integration approach. It handles complex content structures and multi-site setups while staying genuinely usable for marketing and editorial teams day to day.

How important is multisite capability in an enterprise CMS?

For any organisation operating across regions or brands, it's critical. Central control paired with local flexibility is what most enterprise teams are really after – and it's something Craft delivers natively rather than through workarounds.

What are common challenges with enterprise CMS platforms?

Overly complex systems, a lack of editorial flexibility, poor usability for marketing teams and high long-term maintenance costs. These usually trace back to choosing a platform that doesn't match how the business actually operates – which is exactly why the selection process matters as much as the platform itself.

How do you choose the right CMS for enterprise needs?

Start by defining your content structure, workflows and scalability requirements before you look at any platforms. Bring marketing and technical teams in together, judge on long-term fit rather than short-term features, and factor in total cost of ownership rather than the licence fee alone.
