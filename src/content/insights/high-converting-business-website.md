Plenty of websites look great and quietly underperform. Conversion — turning a visitor into an enquiry, booking or sale — comes from a handful of decisions that are easy to overlook.

## 1. Clarity in the first five seconds

A visitor should immediately understand what you do, who it’s for and what to do next. That means a clear headline, a supporting line in plain language and one obvious primary action.

## 2. One primary action per page

When every page asks for five different things, visitors do none of them. Decide the single most valuable action for each page and design towards it. Secondary options can exist — they just shouldn’t compete.

## 3. Speed

Slow pages lose people before they’ve seen anything. Optimised images, lean code and good hosting make a measurable difference to both user experience and search visibility.

## 4. Trust signals in the right places

- Real work and real results, shown honestly.
- Clear contact details and a visible way to get in touch.
- Genuine reviews and recognisable clients, where you have permission to show them.
- Security and privacy information near forms.

> Trust isn’t a section on the homepage. It’s built at every point where someone hesitates.

## 5. Forms that feel easy

Ask only for what you need, explain why, and make the form work perfectly on a phone. A short form that gets completed beats a detailed one that gets abandoned.

## 6. Mobile first, always

For many businesses, most visitors arrive on mobile. Designing for small screens first forces the priorities that make every version of the site better.

## 7. Measure and iterate

Set up analytics and conversion tracking from launch day. Then keep testing headlines, layouts and calls to action — small changes compound over time.
